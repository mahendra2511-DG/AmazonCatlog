3-Week Training Roadmap

Follow this plan day by day. Weeks build on each other: foundation → interview skills → full mock simulations.

Week 1 · Foundation

Spoken English + role knowledge + aptitude
  • Daily 2-minute JAM practice (45 min)
  • Learn the Catalog Specialist role — write it in your own words
  • Learn 4 Leadership Principles with one life example each
  • Aptitude drill: percentages, ratios, reasoning, verbal (1 hr/day)
  • Grammar clinic: tenses, articles, subject-verb agreement
  • Typing practice starts — target 30 WPM, 95% accuracy
  • Excel basics: sort, filter, duplicates, VLOOKUP, pivot intro
  • Day 7 test: JAM + aptitude set + typing

Week 2 · Interview Skills

STAR stories + writing + scenarios
  • Learn STAR method; write 3 personal stories (challenge, mistake, deadline)
  • 150-word essay practice with correction
  • HR question drill: intro, why Amazon, strengths & weaknesses
  • Catalog scenario training: discrepancies, wrong attributes, prioritization
  • Puzzle practice — learn to "think aloud"
  • Versant-style speaking: repeat, answer fast, read aloud
  • Day 14 review: essays + STAR answers checked 1-on-1

Week 3 · Mock Interviews

Full simulation & polish
  • Mock JAM in group setting (with elimination pressure)
  • Timed aptitude test — 40 questions
  • Essay + typing + Versant simulation
  • Mock technical / ops interview
  • Mock manager + HR interview (record & review body language)
  • Feedback day: personal scorecard + 3 improvement points
  • Day 21: all rounds back-to-back, exactly like the real one-day drive

⚡ Daily non-negotiables (all 21 days)

45 min JAM speaking · 30 min typing · 30 min aptitude. Consistency beats intensity — small daily reps are what remove hesitation in the real interview.

The Interview, Step by Step

Based on real candidate reports the process usually runs in this order — often completed in a single day. Tap each step to see what is tested and how to prepare.

1

JAM / Extempore / Group Discussion Heavy elimination▾

Speak 2–5 minutes on a given topic, sometimes choosing from a topic list in a group. One candidate reported over half the group was eliminated here.

They check: fluency, confidence, structured thinking.
Winning frame: one opening line → 2–3 points → one closing line. No long pauses, no mixing Hindi mid-sentence.
2

Aptitude / Online Test ▾

Typically ~40 easy questions: 20 English (grammar, vocabulary, comprehension) + 20 reasoning (logical, non-verbal, percentages, ratios, data interpretation). Some candidates got a Watson Glaser critical-thinking test or scenario MCQs about cataloging instead.

They check: basics under time pressure. Prep: timed IndiaBix-style sets — speed matters more than difficulty.
3

Versant Test — Speaking + Typing ▾

A computer-based English test: repeat sentences, answer short questions, read passages aloud, respond to prompts — plus a typing test of moderate difficulty.

They check: pronunciation, fluency, listening, typing speed/accuracy. Prep: practice free Versant samples online; speak clearly at natural speed, don't rush.
4

Essay Writing ▾

A short essay (~150 words, sometimes up to 2 pages) on one of 2–3 topics.

They check: written grammar, spelling, organization. Fixed structure to use: 2-line intro → 2 short body paragraphs (one point + one example each) → 2-line conclusion. No SMS spellings.
5

Technical / Operations Interview ▾

Questions on the Catalog Specialist role, data handling, Excel/SQL, and catalog scenarios — wrong attributes, discrepancies, ambiguous products.

They check: role understanding, attention to detail, structured problem solving. All these questions are in the Q&A Bank → Role & Technical and Scenarios categories.
6

Manager / Behavioral Interview ▾

STAR-based questions — challenges, conflicts, deadlines, prioritization — plus puzzles in some interviews (tennis balls in a car, organizing an unlabeled library).

They check: Leadership Principles fit, calm thinking, communication. Rule for puzzles: your approach matters, not the exact answer — think aloud.
7

HR Round ▾

Career goals, why Amazon, salary expectation, culture fit, shift/relocation willingness.

They check: motivation, stability, honesty. Never criticize a previous college/employer, and never bluff — "I'm not sure, but my approach would be…" is a respected answer.
